## Overview
A slice across the body of the hippocampus. The signal enters from the entorhinal cortex, passes through the dentate gyrus, CA3 and CA1, and leaves through the subiculum back to the entorhinal cortex: the loop often called the trisynaptic circuit [amaral1989]. Each cell is drawn from a real traced neuron, scaled to fit its layer. The traced shapes come from NeuroMorpho.Org [tecuatl2024].

## Cell granule
Small round cells packed tightly in the granule cell layer of the dentate gyrus. Their dendrites spread into the molecular layer, where they receive the perforant path from layer II of the entorhinal cortex. Their axons, the mossy fibres, cross the hilus and end on CA3 pyramidal cells [amaral1989]. The traced cells shown are adult rat granule cells [oreilly2018].

## Cell ca3-pyramidal
Large pyramidal cells. Mossy fibres end on the first part of their apical dendrite, in the stratum lucidum. Their recurrent collaterals connect CA3 pyramidal cells to each other, and their Schaffer collaterals carry the signal on to CA1 [amaral1989]. The traced cells shown are adult rat CA3 pyramidal cells [ishizuka1995].

## Cell ca1-pyramidal
Pyramidal cells whose dendrites receive the Schaffer collaterals from CA3 in the strata radiatum and oriens, and direct input from layer III of the entorhinal cortex in the stratum lacunosum moleculare. They are the main output of the hippocampus, to the subiculum and the entorhinal cortex [amaral1989]. The traced cells shown are adult human CA1 cells, which are larger than mouse CA1 cells and differ in structure [benavides2019].

## Cell basket
Inhibitory interneurons that contain parvalbumin. Their axons wrap the cell bodies of pyramidal and granule cells, so they control when those cells fire [freund1996]. They help time the activity of the whole circuit during brain rhythms [klausberger2008]. The traced cells shown are rat basket cells from CA1 and the dentate gyrus [forro2015] [degro2022].

## Cell olm
Inhibitory interneurons whose cell bodies lie in the stratum oriens of CA1. CA1 pyramidal cells excite them, and their axons inhibit the far ends of the pyramidal cell dendrites in the stratum lacunosum moleculare, where the entorhinal input arrives [klausberger2008]. The traced cell shown is an adult rat O-LM cell [katona2014].

## Cell hipp
Inhibitory interneurons in the hilus, called hilar perforant path associated (HIPP) cells. Their axons reach the outer molecular layer, where the perforant path arrives on granule cell dendrites, so they act like the O-LM cells of the dentate gyrus [freund1996]. The traced cell shown is a rat HIPP cell [degro2022].

## Layer ca1-pyramidale
The layer of CA1 pyramidal cell bodies [amaral1989].

## Layer ca2-pyramidale
CA2, a narrow zone of pyramidal cells between CA3 and CA1 [amaral1989].

## Layer ca3-pyramidale
The layer of CA3 pyramidal cell bodies. Its end curves into the hilus of the dentate gyrus [amaral1989].

## Layer oriens
The layer of basal dendrites of pyramidal cells, between the cell bodies and the white matter; many interneurons, such as O-LM cells, sit here [amaral1989] [klausberger2008].

## Layer lucidum
A thin layer of CA3, just above the cell bodies, where the mossy fibres run and end [amaral1989].

## Layer radiatum
The layer of the apical dendrites of pyramidal cells, where the Schaffer collaterals and CA3 recurrent collaterals arrive [amaral1989].

## Layer lacunosum-moleculare
The deepest layer of CA1, holding the far ends of the apical dendrites, where fibres from layer III of the entorhinal cortex end [amaral1989].

## Layer dg-granule
The tightly packed C shaped layer of granule cell bodies [amaral1989].

## Layer dg-molecular
The layer of granule cell dendrites, where the perforant path from the entorhinal cortex arrives [amaral1989].

## Layer dg-hilus
The hilus, or polymorphic layer, inside the C of the granule layer. Mossy fibres cross it on their way to CA3, and it holds several kinds of interneurons [amaral1989] [freund1996].

## Layer fissure
The hippocampal fissure, the boundary between the molecular layer of the dentate gyrus and the stratum lacunosum moleculare of CA1 [amaral1989].

## Box ec
The entorhinal cortex, the main gateway between the neocortex and the hippocampus. Its layer II sends the perforant path to the dentate gyrus and CA3; its layer III projects to CA1 and the subiculum; its deep layers receive the output of the hippocampus [amaral1989].

## Box subiculum
The subiculum receives the output of CA1 and sends it on to the deep layers of the entorhinal cortex and to other targets [amaral1989].

## Basic step 1
The entorhinal cortex sends the signal along the perforant path to the granule cells.

## Basic step 2
Granule cells pass it along the mossy fibres to CA3 pyramidal cells.

## Basic step 3
CA3 pyramidal cells pass it along the Schaffer collaterals to CA1 pyramidal cells.

## Basic step 4
CA1 pyramidal cells send it out to the subiculum and back to the entorhinal cortex.

## Full step 1
The perforant path from entorhinal layer II reaches the granule cells and also CA3.

## Full step 2
Mossy fibres excite CA3 pyramidal cells and, in the hilus, basket cells and HIPP cells.

## Full step 3
Basket cells inhibit the granule cell bodies; HIPP cells inhibit their outer dendrites.

## Full step 4
CA3 pyramidal cells excite each other through recurrent collaterals.

## Full step 5
Schaffer collaterals excite CA1 pyramidal cells, while basket cells inhibit them around the cell bodies.

## Full step 6
Entorhinal layer III reaches the far dendrites of CA1 directly, along the temporoammonic path.

## Full step 7
CA1 pyramidal cells excite O-LM cells, which inhibit the far ends of CA1 dendrites.

## Full step 8
CA1 pyramidal cells send the signal out to the subiculum and back to the entorhinal cortex.
