## Overview
The suprachiasmatic nucleus is the master clock: its neurons keep a roughly 24-hour rhythm and are reset each day by light. It times melatonin at night and the morning cortisol rise, and works with arousal centres and a sleep switch in the hypothalamus [saper2005].

## Step 1
Role: Light-sensitive retinal ganglion cells send light signals to the suprachiasmatic nucleus through the retinohypothalamic tract [saper2005].
Disorders: Blindness without any light perception can leave the clock running out of step with the day [kandel2021].

## Step 2
Role: The suprachiasmatic nucleus keeps the 24-hour rhythm and resets it from the light signal [saper2005].
Disorders: Jet lag and shift work put the clock out of step with the day; delayed sleep phase shifts it later [kandel2021].
Drugs: Melatonin taken in the evening can shift the clock earlier [saper2005].

## Step 3
Role: The paraventricular nucleus passes the clock signal to the sympathetic pathway that controls the pineal gland, and times the morning cortisol rise [saper2005].
Disorders: A flattened cortisol rhythm is common in depression and chronic stress [kandel2021].

## Step 4
Role: The pineal gland releases melatonin at night, signalling darkness to the body [saper2005].
Disorders: Bright light at night suppresses melatonin and can delay sleep [kandel2021].
Drugs: Melatonin and melatonin agonists help some sleep-onset and jet-lag problems [kandel2021].

## Step 5
Role: Orexin (hypocretin) neurons of the lateral hypothalamus keep wakefulness stable [saper2005].
Disorders: Their loss causes narcolepsy, with sleep attacks and cataplexy [thannickal2000].
Drugs: Orexin receptor blockers such as suvorexant are used for insomnia [kandel2021].

## Step 6
Role: The tuberomammillary nucleus (histamine), locus coeruleus (noradrenaline) and raphe (serotonin) form the arousal system that keeps the cortex awake [saper2005].
Disorders: Weak arousal signalling contributes to daytime sleepiness [saper2005].
Drugs: Older antihistamines cause drowsiness by blocking histamine; modafinil and caffeine promote wakefulness [kandel2021].

## Step 7
Role: The thalamus and cortex switch from slow sleep rhythms to waking activity under this arousal input [saper2005].
Disorders: Damage to the arousal input of the thalamus can cause long-lasting loss of consciousness [kandel2021].

## Step 8
Role: The ventrolateral preoptic nucleus inhibits the arousal centres to switch on sleep, and they in turn inhibit it, forming a flip-flop switch [saper2005].
Disorders: Its loss with age is linked to broken sleep [saper2005].
Drugs: Benzodiazepine-type sleeping pills enhance GABA inhibition, the signal this nucleus uses [kandel2021].
