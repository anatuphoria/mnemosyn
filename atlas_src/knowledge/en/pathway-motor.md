## Overview
The corticospinal tract carries commands for voluntary movement from the motor cortex to the spinal cord. It runs down on its own side and crosses at the lower end of the medulla, so each hemisphere moves the opposite side of the body [purves2018].

## Step 1
Role: The primary motor cortex is mapped by body part; its large pyramidal neurons start the corticospinal tract [penfield1937].
Lesion: Weakness of the {opposite} side of the body, mostly of the face and arm or of the leg depending on the part damaged, with upper motor neuron signs [kandel2021].

## Step 2
Role: Descends through the internal capsule, the cerebral peduncle, the pons and the medullary pyramid on the {same} side [purves2018].
Lesion: A lesion above the crossing, for example in the internal capsule, causes weakness of the {opposite} side of the body with spasticity, brisk reflexes and an upgoing plantar response [kandel2021].

## Step 3
Role: At the junction of the medulla and the spinal cord most corticospinal fibres cross to the {opposite} side [purves2018].
Lesion: Above this point a lesion weakens the side of the body opposite to it, below this point the side of the lesion [purves2018].

## Step 4
Role: Runs down the {opposite} lateral column of the spinal cord to the motor neurons of the {opposite} limbs [purves2018].
Lesion: Weakness below the lesion on the {opposite} side of the body, which is the side of the lesion, with upper motor neuron signs, as in Brown-Sequard syndrome [kandel2021].
