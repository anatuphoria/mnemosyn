## Overview
Deciding and controlling oneself depend on the prefrontal cortex, which holds goals in mind and biases other areas toward them, working in loops with the striatum and thalamus [miller2001].

## Step 1
Role: The dorsolateral prefrontal cortex holds goals and rules in working memory and plans steps toward them [miller2001].
Disorders: Damage impairs planning and flexible thinking; reduced function is reported in schizophrenia [kandel2021].
Drugs: Methylphenidate and atomoxetine improve prefrontal control in ADHD [kandel2021].

## Step 2
Role: The orbitofrontal cortex judges the value of options and updates it when outcomes change [kandel2021].
Disorders: Overactivity in orbitofrontal-striatal loops is linked to obsessive-compulsive disorder [kandel2021].
Drugs: High-dose SSRIs reduce the symptoms of obsessive-compulsive disorder [kandel2021].

## Step 3
Role: The ventromedial prefrontal cortex links decisions to emotions and social rules [damasio1994].
Disorders: Phineas Gage became impulsive and socially careless after an iron bar damaged this region in 1848 [damasio1994].

## Step 4
Role: The anterior cingulate cortex detects conflicts and errors and calls for more control [miller2001].
Disorders: Altered anterior cingulate function is reported in ADHD and obsessive-compulsive disorder [kandel2021].

## Step 5
Role: The inferior frontal gyrus, especially on the right, helps stop actions that are no longer wanted [kandel2021].
Disorders: Weak stopping is linked to impulsivity in ADHD and addiction [kandel2021].

## Step 6
Role: The mediodorsal nucleus of the thalamus links the prefrontal cortex with the basal ganglia loop and keeps goal information active [miller2001].
Disorders: Damage here can cause apathy and memory problems similar to frontal damage [kandel2021].

## Step 7
Role: The caudate and nucleus accumbens weigh effort against reward and turn choices into action [schultz1997].
Disorders: Addiction biases these loops toward drug rewards over long-term goals [kandel2021].
