## Overview
Acetylcholine in the brain comes from the basal forebrain, which supplies the cortex and hippocampus, and from the brainstem, which supplies the thalamus. It raises attention to new information and helps store new memories [hasselmo2006].

## Step 1
Role: The nucleus basalis of Meynert is the main source of acetylcholine for the cortex [hasselmo2006].
Disorders: It degenerates early in Alzheimer's disease, and its loss contributes to the memory decline [kandel2021].
Drugs: Donepezil and other cholinesterase inhibitors slow the breakdown of acetylcholine and modestly help memory in Alzheimer's disease [kandel2021].

## Step 2
Role: Across the cortex acetylcholine strengthens incoming sensory signals over internal activity, which supports attention [hasselmo2006].
Disorders: Loss of cortical acetylcholine contributes to inattention and confusion in dementia [kandel2021].
Drugs: Nicotine acts on cortical nicotinic receptors and briefly sharpens attention [kandel2021].

## Step 3
Role: The medial septal nucleus supplies the hippocampus with acetylcholine and paces its theta rhythm [hasselmo2006].
Disorders: Damage to the septum impairs memory for places [kandel2021].

## Step 4
Role: In the hippocampus acetylcholine favours storing new memories over recalling old ones [hasselmo2006].
Disorders: Low hippocampal acetylcholine contributes to forgetfulness in Alzheimer's disease [kandel2021].
Drugs: Anticholinergic medicines such as scopolamine impair new memories and can cause delirium in older people [kandel2021].

## Step 5
Role: The pedunculopontine and laterodorsal tegmental nuclei of the brainstem release acetylcholine onto the thalamus during waking and dream sleep [saper2005].
Disorders: Their loss in Parkinson's disease is linked to falls and gait problems [kandel2021].

## Step 6
Role: In the thalamus acetylcholine helps open the relay to the cortex during wakefulness and REM sleep [saper2005].
Disorders: Anticholinergic overload disturbs this relay and contributes to delirium [kandel2021].
