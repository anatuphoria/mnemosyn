## Overview
The spinothalamic pathway carries pain and temperature. Its fibres cross the spinal cord within a segment or two of where they enter, then run up the opposite side to the thalamus and the somatosensory cortex [purves2018].

## Step 1
Role: Fibres for pain and temperature enter the spinal cord and end on neurons of the {same} dorsal horn near their level of entry [purves2018].
Lesion: Loss of pain and temperature in the area supplied by the damaged segments, on the {same} side [kandel2021].

## Step 2
Role: Axons of dorsal horn neurons cross in front of the central canal within one or two segments and form the spinothalamic tract [purves2018].
Lesion: A lesion around the central canal, for example syringomyelia, cuts the crossing fibres and causes loss of pain and temperature on both sides in a cape over the shoulders, with touch kept [kandel2021].

## Step 3
Role: Carries pain and temperature from the {same} side of the body up the {opposite} side of the spinal cord and brainstem to the thalamus [purves2018].
Lesion: Loss of pain and temperature on the {same} side of the body, starting one or two segments below a spinal lesion; in Brown-Sequard syndrome this is the side opposite the weakness [kandel2021].

## Step 4
Role: The {opposite} ventral posterolateral nucleus relays pain and temperature to the somatosensory cortex [purves2018].
Lesion: Loss of sensation on the {same} side of the body, sometimes followed by severe burning pain there (thalamic pain syndrome) [kandel2021].

## Step 5
Role: Places pain and temperature on the body map [penfield1937].
Lesion: Poorer locating of pain and temperature on the {same} side of the body; the feeling of pain itself is often kept [kandel2021].
