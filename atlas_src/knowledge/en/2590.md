## Function
A small nucleus above the optic chiasm that is the body's master clock. It keeps a rhythm of about twenty four hours and sets the daily cycles of sleep, hormones and body temperature [saper2005].

## Connections
It receives light information from the retina through the retinohypothalamic tract and projects to other hypothalamic nuclei, which pass the rhythm on to the pineal gland and the stress axis [saper2005].

## Clinical
Disturbing the clock, as in jet lag or shift work, shifts the cortisol rhythm [weitzman1971].
