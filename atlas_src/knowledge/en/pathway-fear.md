## Overview
The fear circuit centres on the amygdala. A fast route from the sensory thalamus and a slower one through the cortex bring signals of danger; the amygdala then drives freezing, stress hormones and arousal, while the hippocampus adds context and the prefrontal cortex calms the response [ledoux2000].

## Step 1
Role: The medial geniculate nucleus sends a fast, rough copy of a sound straight to the amygdala, before the cortex has analysed it [ledoux2000].
Disorders: This quick route lets learned danger cues trigger fear before they are consciously recognised [ledoux2000].

## Step 2
Role: The amygdala learns which cues predict danger; its central nucleus sends the commands for the fear response [ledoux2000].
Disorders: An overactive amygdala is linked to anxiety disorders and PTSD; damage to both amygdalae reduces fear [kandel2021].
Drugs: Benzodiazepines enhance GABA inhibition in the amygdala and reduce anxiety [kandel2021].

## Step 3
Role: The lateral hypothalamus raises heart rate and blood pressure through the autonomic system, and the paraventricular nucleus starts the stress hormones [ledoux2000].
Disorders: Repeated activation contributes to the bodily symptoms of chronic anxiety [kandel2021].

## Step 4
Role: The periaqueductal grey in the midbrain organises freezing and, under greater threat, fight or flight [ledoux2000].
Disorders: Its activation is thought to underlie the intense bodily feelings of panic attacks [kandel2021].

## Step 5
Role: The locus coeruleus raises vigilance and arousal during fear [astonjones2005].
Disorders: Persistent hyperarousal in PTSD is linked to this system [kandel2021].
Drugs: Prazosin, which blocks noradrenaline alpha-1 receptors, is used for PTSD nightmares [kandel2021].

## Step 6
Role: The hippocampus tells the amygdala where and when danger occurred, so fear fits the context [ledoux2000].
Disorders: Weak context signalling may let fear spread to safe situations in PTSD [kandel2021].

## Step 7
Role: The medial prefrontal and subgenual cingulate cortex restrain the amygdala when a cue is no longer dangerous (extinction) [ledoux2000].
Disorders: Weak prefrontal control of the amygdala is linked to persistent anxiety and PTSD [kandel2021].
Drugs: Exposure therapy relies on this extinction learning; SSRIs support it in PTSD [kandel2021].
