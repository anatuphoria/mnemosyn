## Overview
In 1937 James Papez proposed a loop from the hippocampus through the fornix, the mammillary body, the anterior thalamus, the cingulate cortex and the parahippocampal gyrus back to the hippocampus as the basis of emotion [papez1937]. Today it is seen mainly as a memory circuit: damage at several of its stations causes amnesia [kandel2021].

## Step 1
Role: The hippocampus binds new experiences into lasting memories and sends its output through the fornix [purves2018].
Lesion: One-sided damage causes a mild memory deficit, for words with a left lesion and for places and pictures with a right one; damage on both sides prevents new memories from forming [scoville1957].

## Step 2
Role: The main output bundle of the hippocampus; it arches under the corpus callosum to the mammillary body [purves2018].
Lesion: Damage to both fornices, for example during surgery for a colloid cyst of the third ventricle, can cause lasting difficulty forming new memories [kandel2021].

## Step 3
Role: A hypothalamic nucleus that receives the fornix and relays to the anterior thalamus through the mammillothalamic tract [purves2018].
Lesion: Damaged on both sides in thiamine deficiency (Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome), with severe memory loss and confabulation [kandel2021].

## Step 4
Role: Carries signals from the mammillary body to the anterior nuclei of the thalamus [purves2018].
Lesion: Thalamic strokes that cut this tract can cause memory loss like that of mammillary body damage [kandel2021].

## Step 5
Role: Part of the anterior thalamic nuclei; it relays the circuit to the cingulate cortex [purves2018].
Lesion: Damage to the anterior thalamus can cause amnesia [kandel2021].

## Step 6
Role: A long association bundle inside the cingulate gyrus that carries the circuit around the corpus callosum toward the parahippocampal gyrus [papez1937].
Lesion: Surgical cuts of its front part (cingulotomy) have been used for intractable pain; they lessen the distress pain causes [kandel2021].

## Step 7
Role: Papez placed the experience of emotion in the cingulate cortex; its middle and posterior parts pass the circuit on through the cingulum [papez1937].
Lesion: Damage to the posterior cingulate cortex can impair memory and finding the way [kandel2021].

## Step 8
Role: Contains the entorhinal cortex, the main gateway back into the hippocampus, which closes the loop [purves2018].
Lesion: The entorhinal cortex is among the first areas damaged in Alzheimer's disease, an early cause of memory loss [braak1991].

## Step 9
Role: The circuit returns to the hippocampus through the perforant path from the entorhinal cortex [purves2018].
Lesion: As at the start: damage on both sides prevents new memories from forming [scoville1957].
