## Overview
The dorsal column and medial lemniscus pathway carries fine touch, vibration and position sense. It runs up the spinal cord on the side it enters, crosses in the medulla and reaches the thalamus and the somatosensory cortex of the opposite hemisphere [purves2018].

## Step 1
Role: Fibres for fine touch, vibration and position sense run up the {same} dorsal column, gracile for the leg and cuneate for the arm, to their nuclei in the medulla [purves2018].
Lesion: Loss of fine touch, vibration and position sense on the {same} side below the lesion, with unsteadiness that worsens with the eyes closed [kandel2021].

## Step 2
Role: Axons of the gracile and cuneate nuclei cross the midline of the medulla and form the medial lemniscus [purves2018].
Lesion: From here on a lesion causes loss on the side of the body opposite to it [purves2018].

## Step 3
Role: Carries touch and position sense from the {same} side of the body up the {opposite} brainstem to the thalamus [purves2018].
Lesion: Loss of fine touch, vibration and position sense on the {same} side of the body, for example in medial medullary syndrome [kandel2021].

## Step 4
Role: The {opposite} ventral posterolateral nucleus is the thalamic relay for body sensation and projects to the primary somatosensory cortex [purves2018].
Lesion: Loss of sensation on the {same} side of the body, sometimes followed by severe pain on that side (thalamic pain syndrome) [kandel2021].

## Step 5
Role: The primary somatosensory cortex, mapped by body part [penfield1937].
Lesion: Loss of fine touch and position sense on the {same} side of the body, with difficulty recognising objects by touch [kandel2021].
