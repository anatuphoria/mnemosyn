## Overview
Sound moves the hair cells of the cochlea, whose signals travel through the cochlear nerve to the cochlear nuclei, then up both sides of the brainstem through the superior olivary complex, the lateral lemniscus and the inferior colliculus to the medial geniculate nucleus and the primary auditory cortex. Because the pathway crosses early and on both sides, each hemisphere hears both ears [purves2018].

## Step 1
Role: The cochlear part of the {same} vestibulocochlear nerve carries sound signals from the hair cells of the {same} cochlea to the cochlear nuclei [purves2018].
Lesion: Hearing loss in the {same} ear, often with tinnitus; a vestibular schwannoma here also causes imbalance [kandel2021].

## Step 2
Role: The {same} cochlear nuclei receive only the {same} ear, so this is the last station where each ear is heard alone [purves2018].
Lesion: Deafness in the {same} ear; this is the only level of the brain where a one-sided lesion causes one-sided deafness [kandel2021].

## Step 3
Role: Most fibres from the cochlear nuclei cross the pons here toward the {opposite} superior olivary complex and lateral lemniscus, while some stay on the {same} side, so from here on each side carries both ears [purves2018].
Lesion: Damage here mainly impairs locating sounds, because the superior olivary complex compares the timing and loudness at the two ears [purves2018].

## Step 4
Role: Carries signals from both ears, mostly the {same} one, up the {opposite} brainstem to the inferior colliculus [purves2018].
Lesion: A one-sided lesion above the cochlear nuclei rarely causes deafness, because each side carries both ears; locating sounds can suffer [kandel2021].

## Step 5
Role: The {opposite} inferior colliculus integrates differences in timing and loudness between the ears and maps where a sound comes from [purves2018].
Lesion: Usually mild difficulty locating sounds; lesions on both sides can cause marked hearing loss [kandel2021].

## Step 6
Role: The {opposite} medial geniculate nucleus is the thalamic relay for hearing and projects to the primary auditory cortex [purves2018].
Lesion: Little or no hearing loss from one side, with difficulty locating sounds and following speech in noise [kandel2021].

## Step 7
Role: Carries auditory signals from the {opposite} medial geniculate nucleus to the {opposite} Heschl gyrus [purves2018].
Lesion: No deafness from one side, but poorer locating of sounds coming from the {same} side [kandel2021].

## Step 8
Role: The primary auditory cortex, mapped by sound frequency [purves2018].
Lesion: A one-sided lesion does not cause deafness, because each cortex hears both ears; it impairs locating sounds on the {same} side. Lesions on both sides cause cortical deafness [kandel2021].
