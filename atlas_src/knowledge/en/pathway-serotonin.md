## Overview
Serotonin comes from the raphe nuclei of the brainstem, whose axons reach almost the whole brain. It shapes mood, anxiety, impulse control, appetite and sleep, and most antidepressants act on it [berger2009].

## Step 1
Role: The dorsal and median raphe nuclei hold most of the serotonin neurons that project to the forebrain [berger2009].
Disorders: Low serotonin signalling is linked to depression, anxiety and impulsive aggression, although no single cause has been proven [kandel2021].
Drugs: SSRIs block the serotonin transporter and raise serotonin around raphe targets; their effect on mood usually takes weeks [kandel2021].

## Step 2
Role: Median raphe input to the hippocampus helps regulate memory and the response to stress [berger2009].
Disorders: In chronic stress and depression the hippocampus can shrink; antidepressant treatment may help it recover [kandel2021].

## Step 3
Role: Serotonin in the amygdala dampens or sharpens responses to threat [berger2009].
Disorders: Anxiety disorders are linked to an overactive amygdala that serotonin normally helps to restrain [kandel2021].
Drugs: SSRIs are first-line medicines for many anxiety disorders [kandel2021].

## Step 4
Role: The subgenual and pregenual cingulate and medial prefrontal cortex, rich in serotonin receptors, help regulate mood [berger2009].
Disorders: The subgenual cingulate is overactive in depression [kandel2021].
Drugs: Psilocybin and LSD act on serotonin 2A receptors in the cortex and change perception and mood [berger2009].

## Step 5
Role: Serotonin in the striatum and nucleus accumbens restrains impulsive and repetitive behaviour [berger2009].
Disorders: Obsessive-compulsive disorder is linked to these circuits [kandel2021].
Drugs: High-dose SSRIs and clomipramine are used in obsessive-compulsive disorder [kandel2021].

## Step 6
Role: Raphe input to the suprachiasmatic nucleus links serotonin to the body clock and sleep [saper2005].
Disorders: Sleep is often disturbed in depression, with early waking [kandel2021].
