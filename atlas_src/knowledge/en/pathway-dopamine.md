## Overview
Dopamine neurons in the midbrain send three main projections: from the ventral tegmental area to the nucleus accumbens and limbic areas, to the prefrontal cortex, and from the substantia nigra to the dorsal striatum. Their bursts signal rewards that are better than expected, which drives learning and motivation [schultz1997].

## Step 1
Role: Dopamine neurons of the ventral tegmental area fire in bursts when a reward is better than predicted and pause when it is worse, a teaching signal for learning what to seek [schultz1997].
Disorders: Overactive signalling in this system is linked to the positive symptoms of schizophrenia and to the craving of addiction [kandel2021].
Drugs: Opioids, alcohol and nicotine raise the firing of these neurons, part of why they are addictive [kandel2021].

## Step 2
Role: The ascending dopamine axons travel forward through the lateral hypothalamus in the medial forebrain bundle [purves2018].
Disorders: Damage along this bundle can cause apathy and loss of motivation [kandel2021].

## Step 3
Role: The nucleus accumbens, the ventral striatum, turns reward signals into wanting and approach behaviour [schultz1997].
Disorders: Addiction changes this nucleus so that drug cues drive compulsive seeking [kandel2021].
Drugs: Cocaine and amphetamine raise dopamine here by blocking or reversing its reuptake; antipsychotics block D2 receptors here [kandel2021].

## Step 4
Role: Dopamine in the prefrontal and anterior cingulate cortex tunes working memory and goal-directed thinking [miller2001].
Disorders: Too little dopamine here is linked to the negative and cognitive symptoms of schizophrenia and to ADHD [kandel2021].
Drugs: Methylphenidate raises dopamine and noradrenaline here, which helps attention in ADHD [kandel2021].

## Step 5
Role: Dopamine in the amygdala and hippocampus links places and cues with rewards and makes rewarding events easier to remember [kandel2021].
Disorders: Cue memories stored here keep the risk of relapse high long after drug use stops [kandel2021].

## Step 6
Role: Dopamine neurons of the substantia nigra pars compacta supply the dorsal striatum for starting and smoothing movement [purves2018].
Disorders: Their loss causes Parkinson's disease: slowness, rigidity and rest tremor, often with low mood [kandel2021].
Drugs: L-DOPA is turned into dopamine by the remaining neurons; dopamine agonists act on the striatal receptors directly [kandel2021].

## Step 7
Role: The caudate and putamen receive nigral dopamine, which favours the pathway that releases wanted movements and habits [purves2018].
Disorders: Blocking dopamine here causes parkinsonism and late involuntary movements (tardive dyskinesia) [kandel2021].
Drugs: Antipsychotics that block D2 receptors here cause these movement side effects [kandel2021].
