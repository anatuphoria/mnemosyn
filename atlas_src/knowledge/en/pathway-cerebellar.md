## Overview
The cerebellar loop runs from the motor cortex through the pons to the opposite cerebellar hemisphere, and back through the dentate nucleus, the red nucleus region and the thalamus to the motor cortex it started from. Because the loop crosses twice, each cerebellar hemisphere coordinates the limbs on its own side [purves2018].

## Step 1
Role: The motor cortex sends a copy of its commands to the cerebellum through the pons [purves2018].
Lesion: Weakness of the {opposite} side of the body [kandel2021].

## Step 2
Role: Carries cortical signals down the {same} cerebral peduncle to the pontine nuclei [purves2018].
Lesion: Rarely damaged alone; lesions of the cerebral peduncle also cut the corticospinal tract and weaken the {opposite} side [kandel2021].

## Step 3
Role: Relay neurons in the {same} half of the basal pons; their axons cross the midline [purves2018].
Lesion: Small pontine strokes can cause weakness with clumsiness of the {opposite} limbs (ataxic hemiparesis) [kandel2021].

## Step 4
Role: Pontine fibres cross the pons and enter the {opposite} cerebellar hemisphere through the middle cerebellar peduncle [purves2018].
Lesion: Clumsiness of the limbs on the side of the damaged peduncle [kandel2021].

## Step 5
Role: The lateral cerebellum compares the planned movement with what is happening and times and coordinates it [purves2018].
Lesion: Clumsiness of the {opposite} limbs, the side of the lesion: poor aim with tremor near the target, trouble with rapid alternating movements and an unsteady gait [kandel2021].

## Step 6
Role: The deep nucleus of the lateral cerebellum; its output leaves through the superior cerebellar peduncle [purves2018].
Lesion: Clumsiness and intention tremor of the {opposite} limbs [kandel2021].

## Step 7
Role: Dentate fibres leave through the superior cerebellar peduncle and cross in the midbrain to the {same} red nucleus and thalamus [purves2018].
Lesion: Damage below the crossing causes clumsiness on the side of the lesion, above it on the opposite side [kandel2021].

## Step 8
Role: The {same} red nucleus receives the crossed dentate fibres on their way to the thalamus [purves2018].
Lesion: Tremor and clumsiness of the {opposite} limbs, sometimes with an oculomotor palsy on the {same} side (Benedikt syndrome) [kandel2021].

## Step 9
Role: The {same} ventral lateral nucleus relays the cerebellar output to the motor cortex [purves2018].
Lesion: Clumsiness of the {opposite} limbs; a surgical lesion here has been used to stop tremor [kandel2021].

## Step 10
Role: The loop closes: the motor cortex adjusts its commands with the cerebellar output [purves2018].
Lesion: Weakness of the {opposite} side of the body [kandel2021].
