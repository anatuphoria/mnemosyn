## Overview
Hunger and fullness are set mainly in the hypothalamus, which reads hormones from the gut and fat, and in the brainstem, which reads signals from the stomach. Reward and taste areas decide how much we want a particular food [morton2006].

## Step 1
Role: Ghrelin from the empty stomach rises before meals and signals hunger; leptin from fat and insulin from the pancreas signal stored and incoming energy [cummings2001].
Disorders: Lack of leptin causes severe obesity from childhood; most obesity involves leptin resistance instead [morton2006].

## Step 2
Role: The arcuate nucleus holds two opposing groups: AgRP and NPY neurons that drive hunger and POMC neurons that signal fullness [morton2006].
Disorders: Faults in this melanocortin pathway, such as MC4R mutations, are the commonest single-gene cause of obesity [morton2006].
Drugs: GLP-1 medicines such as semaglutide act on the arcuate nucleus and brainstem to reduce appetite [kandel2021].

## Step 3
Role: The paraventricular nucleus receives the arcuate signals and turns them into fullness and energy use [morton2006].
Disorders: Damage to this area, for example by tumours near the hypothalamus, can cause uncontrolled eating and obesity [morton2006].

## Step 4
Role: The lateral hypothalamus drives hunger and food seeking, partly through orexin and MCH neurons [morton2006].
Disorders: Lesions of the lateral hypothalamus cause loss of appetite and weight in animals [morton2006].
Drugs: Cannabinoids increase appetite partly through receptors in the hypothalamus [morton2006].

## Step 5
Role: The ventromedial nucleus contributes to fullness and to the body's response to low blood sugar [morton2006].
Disorders: Damage to the ventromedial hypothalamus causes overeating and obesity (hypothalamic obesity) [kandel2021].

## Step 6
Role: The nucleus of the solitary tract in the brainstem receives vagal signals of stomach stretch and gut hormones and helps end a meal [morton2006].
Disorders: Disturbed gut-brain signals are thought to contribute to eating disorders [kandel2021].
Drugs: GLP-1 medicines also act here and slow the emptying of the stomach [kandel2021].

## Step 7
Role: The ventral tegmental area and nucleus accumbens make tasty food rewarding and drive eating beyond need [morton2006].
Disorders: Food reward is thought to be altered in binge eating and in anorexia nervosa [kandel2021].

## Step 8
Role: The insula holds the primary taste cortex, and the orbitofrontal cortex values a food, a value that falls as we eat it [kandel2021].
Disorders: Altered insula and orbitofrontal responses to food are reported in anorexia nervosa [kandel2021].
