# Why Mnemosyn

Most students learn the nervous system from a mix of books, apps and flashcards. Each of them is
good at something. Mnemosyn does not try to replace them; it joins the parts they leave apart.
{ .lede }

## What students use today

1. **Atlases and textbooks.** Accurate, carefully drawn and still the core of most courses. But
   they are flat: you have to build the three dimensional brain in your head from sections and
   figures, and that is where much of the effort goes.
2. **3D anatomy apps.** Good for turning shapes around, hiding parts and seeing what lies behind
   them. The most polished ones are paid, and they rarely connect a shape to the slices, the
   pathways or the function you are studying.
3. **Flashcard apps.** Spaced repetition is one of the best ways to remember facts, and many
   medical students use it daily. But you write or download the cards yourself, and a card cannot
   show where a structure sits in the brain.
4. **Physiology simulators.** Some courses use lab simulations for nerves, muscle, the heart and
   blood. They usually come through a course, and they rarely cover hormone loops such as the
   stress axis or water balance.
5. **Lesion and localisation tools.** A few apps draw pathways from a list of symptoms. Placing a
   stroke on an arterial territory and seeing what it destroys is hard to find anywhere.

## What Mnemosyn adds

**One anatomy, every view.** The structure you click in 3D is the same structure in the slice, on
the route, in the Lab and in the lesion. You never translate between a figure in one book and a
diagram in another.

**You change things and watch.** The Lab runs real models of two hormone systems, so a disease or a
drug is something you add and then follow hour by hour, not an arrow you memorise.

**From lesion to signs and back.** Place a lesion and see the deficits it causes, or read a
patient's signs and find the lesion. Both use the same cited texts as the pathways.

**Quizzes that build themselves.** Questions come from the atlas, on structures, groups or tracts,
and are scheduled with the same spaced repetition method many flashcard apps use. You write no
cards.

**Sources you can check.** Shapes, numbers and sentences cite where they come from. Where a model
needs an assumption no source covers, the app marks it as an assumption of this project. Every
decision about the project is written down in public.

**Free and offline.** No account, no subscription, no internet connection once it is installed.

## Where it falls short

Be clear about these before you rely on Mnemosyn:

1. **No specialist has reviewed it yet.** It is a personal study project. The texts are marked as
   drafts until a reviewer checks them. See [Sources and review](trust.md).
2. **The shapes come from MRI atlases.** They are true to measured brains but less detailed and
   less polished than the hand made models in paid anatomy apps.
3. **Desktop only.** It runs on Windows and Linux computers, not on phones or tablets.
4. **No cranial nerve nuclei.** The cranial nerves are there, but the nuclei in the brainstem that
   they come from are not, so syndromes of the medulla are not modelled.
5. **No spinal cord in the public download.** The spinal cord comes from an atlas whose authors
   have not yet allowed it to be shared.
